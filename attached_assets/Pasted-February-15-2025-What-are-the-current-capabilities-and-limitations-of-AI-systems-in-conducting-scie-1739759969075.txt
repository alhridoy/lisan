What are the current capabilities and limitations of AI systems in conducting scientific research across different academic disciplines?
Current AI systems excel at structured research tasks in STEM fields but struggle with complex reasoning and qualitative interpretation, particularly in humanities and social sciences.
Abstract
This report examines the capabilities and limitations of AI systems in scientific research based on nine studies published between 2021 and 2025. The included studies comprise literature reviews, mixed-methods studies, and qualitative analyses examining AI applications across academic disciplines.

The studies included found that AI tools can support various research tasks. According to the reviewed studies, AI applications showed utility in literature review processes, peer review assistance, and research workflow automation. One study reported a 73% reduction in peer reviewer selection time when using AI-assisted matching tools. Studies in STEM fields reported higher accuracy rates for AI tools compared to those in humanities and social sciences.

The reviewed literature identified several limitations. Studies consistently reported accuracy issues, including AI hallucinations and variable response quality, particularly for tasks requiring complex reasoning. Technical constraints, such as context window limits and usage restrictions, were documented across multiple studies. Five of the nine studies specifically noted that AI tools performed less reliably in disciplines requiring nuanced interpretation of qualitative data.

Ethical considerations featured prominently in the reviewed studies. Seven studies emphasized the importance of developing clear guidelines for responsible AI use and maintaining transparency in research processes. The studies consistently positioned AI as a supplementary tool rather than a replacement for human expertise, with six studies providing specific frameworks for human-AI collaboration in research settings.

The reviewed studies proposed various implementation approaches, including quality control measures and integration guidelines. These frameworks emphasized AI literacy training, ongoing tool evaluation, and standardized reporting of AI use in research methodologies.

Based on the reviewed literature, while AI tools can enhance certain research tasks, their effective use requires careful attention to limitations, ethical considerations, and the need for human oversight. The studies suggest that future research should examine discipline-specific applications and long-term impacts of AI integration in academic research.

Castillo-Segura et al. (2024) find that AI tools are effective for basic tasks in Systematic Literature Reviews (SLRs), such as paper screening and classification.

Limitations and Challenges
Technical Constraints
Context Window Limits: Identified by Carabantes-Alarcón et al. (2023) as a limitation in reviewing scientific articles.
Information Retrieval: Castillo-Segura et al. (2024) note limitations in full-text information retrieval for AI tools in SLRs.
Usage Restrictions: Daily operation limits reported by Castillo-Segura et al. (2024).
Accuracy and Reliability Issues
Hallucinations: Mentioned by Carabantes-Alarcón et al. (2023) as a limitation in academic review tasks.
Variable Response Quality: Reported by Danler et al. (2024) for free AI tools in literature review.
Contextual Adaptation and Reasoning: Identified by Morande (2023) as challenges for generative AI models.
Source Selection and Transparency: Highlighted by Danler et al. (2024) as a limitation in AI tools for literature review.
Disciplinary Gaps
STEM Focus: Stronger focus on AI applications in STEM fields observed across studies.
Interdisciplinary Challenges: Farber (2024) notes AI's struggle with understanding interdisciplinary work.
Nuanced Interpretation: Ejjami (2024) highlights AI's difficulty with nuanced interpretation, potentially challenging in qualitative-heavy disciplines.
Implementation Framework
Human-AI Collaboration Models
Complementary Roles: Ejjami (2024) emphasizes AI as an augmentation tool rather than a replacement for human researchers.
Oversight in Peer Review: Carabantes-Alarcón et al. (2023) and Farber (2024) highlight the need for human judgment in AI-assisted peer review processes.
Guided Literature Review: Castillo-Segura et al. (2024) and Danler et al. (2024) suggest a model where AI assists in initial search and categorization, with researchers guiding the process.
Writing Assistance: Khalifa et al. describe a model where AI supports various aspects of academic writing, with researchers ensuring academic integrity.
Quality Control Measures
Verification of AI Outputs: Necessary due to potential hallucinations and variable response quality.
Transparency in AI Use: Salman et al. (2025) emphasize the need for ethical use guidelines and clear documentation of AI tool usage.
Cross-checking with Human Expertise: Farber (2024) demonstrates the importance of combining AI suggestions with human judgment.
Disciplinary Calibration: Quality control measures should be tailored to specific academic fields.
Regular Evaluation of AI Tools: Castillo-Segura et al. (2024) note the importance of regular evaluation due to rapid AI evolution.
Best Practices
AI Literacy Training: Salman et al. (2025) recommend enhancing researchers' AI literacy.
Ethical Guidelines: Multiple studies stress the need for ethical frameworks for AI use in academic research.
Interdisciplinary Approach: Fostering collaboration between AI experts and domain specialists to develop more effective AI tools.
Balanced Integration: Khalifa et al. emphasize balancing AI use with human insight.
Continuous Monitoring and Adjustment: Given AI's variable performance, continuous monitoring of AI tool effectiveness is crucial.
Transparency in Reporting: Clearly reporting AI tool use in research methodologies enhances credibility and reproducibility.

These insights highlight both the potential of AI in enhancing academic research processes and the challenges that need to be addressed for its effective and responsible implementation. The variability in AI performance across tasks and disciplines underscores the need for nuanced, context-specific approaches to AI integration in academic research.
